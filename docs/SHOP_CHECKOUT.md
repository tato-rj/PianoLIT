## Payment and delivery

Customers pay PianoLIT through Stripe. Merchandise payments and Printful fulfillment are separate transactions: after Stripe confirms payment, PianoLIT submits the purchased sync variants to Printful, which charges PianoLIT's configured billing method for production and delivery. This is not a split payment or a payout from Printful.

`ShopCheckout` re-resolves prices and variants on the server, validates the destination, obtains Printful shipping options and a cost estimate, and calculates customer tax with Stripe Tax in live mode. Arthur selected **US-only physical delivery** for launch during the November 5 tax review. Production `shop-checkout.countries` is now `['US']`, preserving Printful's US subdivision data; other delivery countries are omitted from the form and rejected by the shared recipient validation. The actual products/address must also return an available shipping rate and successful estimate. Local/test keep the existing offline destination fixtures, and digital billing countries remain independent. This policy was deployed through Git in `9ce9acbb2` with an atomic configuration-cache rebuild; there were no unexpired unpaid merchandise quotes at rollout. The isolated checkout run passed 43 tests / 435 assertions, including the allowlist form, rejected Canadian address, accepted US address and independent digital-country checks. USD is the supported merchandise currency. A quote lasts 15 minutes. Delivery/tax and digital billing/coupon totals update automatically once required fields are valid and editing has paused for one second; there is no Calculate button. Identical input/change notifications are deduplicated, only one quote request runs at a time, and older responses cannot replace newer address details. Changing the destination clears obsolete delivery options. Closing checkout cancels queued work. Provider failures offer a Try again action without a retry loop. Expired or server-invalidated quotes refresh before payment so the customer can review the updated total; an uncertain payment response never silently starts a replacement payment.

`shop_orders` stores an immutable priced snapshot, encrypted contact/address data and durable Stripe/Printful identities. The browser cannot mark an order paid. A successful PaymentIntent must match the stored order, amount, amount received, currency and live/test mode. The signed webhook and minutely reconciliation recover payments when the browser closes before returning. A lost response reuses the same provider identity; it never starts a replacement charge automatically.

Only a verified, paid production order can create a Printful draft. Fulfillment checks its external ID, sync variants, quantities, destination, currency and estimated cost before confirming it. A cost increase or refunded/disputed payment is held for review. Recovery looks up the stable external ID before creating or confirming anything. Local and test orders cannot reach Printful, even if a real token remains installed. Confirmation can incur a Printful charge; do not use it as a connection test.

Paid digital editions use the same modal without shipping and grant one existing account-owned Purchase after verified payment. Their legacy download formats/endpoints, free/member/owned actions and mobile transaction contracts remain available. Coupons use the shop Stripe account; restricted or incompatible coupons are rejected, and a valid 100% coupon grants delivery without a charge. Digital items are purchased individually and do not enter the physical bag. Existing subscription/account ownership, US/Swiss membership routing and signed membership webhooks stay with the membership billing service.

## Merchandise express checkout

The locally implemented merchandise flow offers eligible Apple Pay/Google Pay in the bag, with a native **Checkout with card** link below. That link navigates to `/checkout`, where express payment appears again above separate Contact, Delivery and card-entry sections. Phone checkout uses a collapsed native order summary and a fixed bag checkout action; desktop keeps the full summary. Signed-in customers see their account contact, while guests get an email field and sign-in link. The same `shop.storefront.checkout.payment` partial and merchandise controller power both entry points; bag mode mounts only the wallet, without card fields or automatic full-address quotes. Old bag `?checkout=merchandise` URLs redirect to the checkout page. Digital and membership flows remain independent.

`POST /checkout/wallet-quote` is a throttled, CSRF-protected, session-locked Shop endpoint. `WalletQuote` resolves the session bag's current prices, available provider shipping rates and the shared Stripe Tax calculation using the wallet's limited country/state/city/postal address. It creates no application order, offer reservation, PaymentIntent or Printful order. When a previously approved wallet address or the normal form already supplies matching destination details, the preview can use those real full-address fields and the provider cost estimate; missing private fields are never fabricated.

Wallet address and delivery-option changes use one serialized request lane. Dismissal/disposal invalidate pending results; a canceled wallet cannot confirm a later prepared payment. The confirm event supplies the full contact/address, which populates the ordinary form and goes through the existing full-address quote. Checkout compares the displayed subtotal, shipping, tax, total, currency and delivery choice before preparing payment. Any difference, including a newly identified first-order offer, stops payment and asks the customer to review the updated total and reopen the wallet. The saved address then permits a more precise preview. A session stores only the pending order ID; an owned, unexpired pending order can prefill its encrypted address on the checkout page. Another account, an expired quote or a paid order cannot supply this prefill. Card payment remains available.

These changes are local and require normal Git deployment, route-cache refresh (if used), compiled-view clearing, the two versioned checkout JavaScript files and the shared checkout/Shop styles. There is no migration, credential, domain, tax-registration or fulfillment-activation change. Desktop/390px layout checks use an isolated rendered fixture with explicitly simulated wallet controls; physical-iPhone/live wallet approval remains unverified. Local development still uses HTTP and does not display actual wallets.

## Newsletter first-order discount

The Shop first-visit popup offers **10% off the first paid Shop order** after explicit newsletter signup. It uses the existing PianoLIT News list only; no automatic signup to Free Pick or other lists is added. The offer is automatic at checkout with the subscribed email, including guest merchandise checkout and signed-in eBook/eScore checkout. The guest email is prefilled from the server session. Digital checkout always uses the authenticated account’s email. Browser local storage remembers only boolean popup-seen and successfully-subscribed UI flags; it never authorizes a discount or stores an email. Dismissing the popup leaves a persistent bottom-left “Get 10% off” button across Shop browsing pages, which reopens the same form. The button uses the shared primary finish with square corners and the tag icon. It cannot be dismissed separately. Both the button and modal are suppressed for every signed-in web account and for guests who already subscribed, using confirmed server/session or browser UI state. Logging in never creates newsletter consent or a subscription flag; failed validation does not count as subscribing.

The discount applies to the final product subtotal, including existing catalog sale prices, rounded once to cents and allocated across product lines. Delivery is unchanged and Stripe Tax receives the discounted line amounts. An explicitly entered digital coupon takes precedence and does not stack with the welcome offer. A $28 product receives $2.80 off: with $5 delivery and no tax, the verified payment total is $30.20. Checkout, order receipts, manager order details and merchandise confirmations show the discount separately.

`Services\Shop\FirstOrderDiscount` issues a single durable grant keyed by a normalized email HMAC and checkout mode. Resubscribing, changing browsers or unsubscribing does not reset or revoke an earned grant. Prior paid orders with the same email/account and legacy paid digital purchases make a customer ineligible; free/member downloads do not count. Demo/sandbox purchases do not consume live eligibility. Email matches are used solely for promotion eligibility and never attach guest orders or expose their details. Email aliases/new guest identities cannot establish that two addresses belong to the same person; authenticated account history and reservations also cover account email changes.

The reservation commits before creating a PaymentIntent, so a lost Stripe response cannot release the offer to a second order. An unchanged order retries the same payment. Correcting an address in the same browser can replace an unpaid payment only after Stripe confirms cancellation; active authentication and processing/succeeded payments stay held. Another browser cannot replace an unexpired checkout. Expired reservations recover the original intent with its saved idempotency key and release only after confirmed cancellation. Confirmed successful payments redeem the grant once, including webhook/background reconciliation. Refunds do not restore the offer. Ambiguous provider outcomes remain held for reconciliation rather than enabling another discounted payment. These cancellation/idempotency rules follow [Stripe PaymentIntent cancellation](https://docs.stripe.com/api/payment_intents/cancel) and [idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests).

Deploy `2026_11_04_230000_add_shop_first_order_discounts.php` **before serving the matching code**. It creates `shop_first_order_discounts`, adds hidden lookup/grant fields to `shop_orders` and backfills keyed email hashes from existing encrypted recipients in batches, preserving recipients, ownership, totals and payment state. Pause checkout/signup writes during this code/migration rollout. Keep `APP_KEY` stable, as already required for encrypted addresses. The ledger contains pseudonymous promotion/payment history and does not authorize indefinite retention; align it with the pending order-retention policy. Back up production before migrating; rolling back deletes the grant ledger and must not be used to reset used offers.

The guest-facing Shop modal and corner button always retain the 10% first-order offer; temporary checkout unavailability must not replace them with generic newsletter copy. Newsletter signup still works while checkout is disabled, saving a future live-mode grant in production. Existing payment, tax and fulfillment activation gates remain enforced. This feature does not enable checkout/Printful, change credentials, alter legacy/mobile billing endpoints, or discount membership subscriptions. Deploy with the existing shared-checkout activation requirements below; legacy fallback digital checkout remains unchanged and does not redeem this promotion.

## Operations and limits

Production shows live orders; local shows demo/sandbox orders. This feature introduces no indefinite-retention authorization or automatic unpaid-quote purge. Define retention and account-closure treatment for shop order records before public launch; preserve legally required payment/order history and never delete paid records merely to clear a queue.

## Merchandise emails and customer tracking

There are three merchandise automatic email types, all enabled by default in Administration → Emails, with independent sending switches, HTML/plain-text previews and the existing admin-only preview-send control:

- **Merchandise shipped:** created once per actual package after an authenticated shipment update. Includes its items, carrier/service, tracking number/link, shipped date and provider delivery estimate when available. A split order or replacement package can therefore produce more than one shipping email; duplicate webhook deliveries do not repeat it. Includes the order reference, latest reported delivery date, purchased items, private order link and help contact. Split orders wait for the last package. Webhook replay and polling cannot resend it; existing historical delivered orders are not automatically backfilled. The sender waits for pending confirmation/shipping messages and rechecks that the order is still fully delivered.

Editable bodies are `resources/views/emails/shop/orders/confirmed.blade.php`, `shipped.blade.php` and `delivered.blade.php`. The shared existing transactional email layout supplies their logo, typography, button and footer. Admin preview fixtures never use real purchases or private tracking links.

The account menu now has **Track your orders**, opening the absolute main-site `/profile/orders` page. The profile header always links to both orders and My downloads. These separate pages do not cross-link; the downloads empty state offers only infographics. It shows only the signed-in account's paid merchandise orders, eight per page. New accounts do not inherit retired or guest purchases merely by sharing an email address. ### Printful shipment webhook activation

This uses **Printful v2 signed webhooks**, whose shipment event is named `shipment_sent` (the newer counterpart to the diagram's unsigned v1 `package_shipped`), plus `shipment_delivered`. Catalog management and fulfillment creation keep their existing v1 endpoints. The handler at `POST https://pianolit.com/webhooks/printful` verifies `x-pf-webhook-public-key` and the HMAC-SHA256 `x-pf-webhook-signature` over the exact raw body, using the **hex-decoded** one-time secret. Missing/invalid signatures, wrong stores and unpaid/unrelated orders cannot update a customer order. No legacy unsigned compatibility bypass is provided.

After authentication it re-reads the saved external order identity through the server-only API, verifies order/shipment identity and item quantities, and copies bounded tracking fields. V2's shipment endpoint adds delivery status, estimates and recent carrier events to the v1 shipped-package data. Links require safe HTTPS URLs; arbitrary callback data never becomes a carrier link. Duplicate receipts and unique order/package email keys protect retries. Provider failures or a shipment not yet visible through the API return 503 for retry. Delivery updates cannot be overwritten by older shipped notifications.

Before enabling this on the server:

1. Deploy through Git and run `2026_10_28_220000_add_shop_order_tracking.php` after the original shop-order migration. This adds tracking fields, `shop_shipments`, `shop_order_emails` and minimal `printful_webhook_receipts` without raw webhook/customer payloads. This migration is installed locally only. Refresh the configuration cache and clear/rebuild route and compiled-view caches before registering the callback (`config:cache`, `route:clear`, `view:clear`, then the deployment’s usual route caching if used).
2. Verify the real store token has Orders read access and permission to configure webhooks, in addition to the previously required order-write/catalog permissions. The installed product-read-only token is insufficient for the complete workflow. Confirm the public HTTPS callback route is deployed before registering it.
3. Run `php artisan printful:webhooks` to inspect settings without changing them. Run `php artisan printful:webhooks --configure` explicitly to register the two shipment events and save the returned signing key pair in the ignored `storage/app/private/printful-webhook-keys.json` (override its path with `PRINTFUL_WEBHOOK_KEYS_FILE`). The command refuses local execution and refuses to replace a different callback or unrelated event configuration. Its repeated run is a no-op when matching keys/events already exist. Existing settings must be reviewed before replacing them; never overwrite another consumer silently.
4. The signing secret is returned once. The command stages and atomically installs a mode-0600 file, never printing its values. Grant the PHP worker and scheduler identities read access using the established private-directory/read ACL pattern. Atomic replacement creates a new file, so reapply a named PHP read ACL after rotation. Then verify `printful:webhooks` from the actual worker identity. No webhook expiry is requested; the API token's separate expiry/rotation still applies. Printful signing-key changes require the matching local private key file immediately.
5. Run `shop:send-order-emails` every minute and `shop:sync-shipments` every 15 minutes, with overlap protection; both are registered in Kernel. The existing catalog-only and GA-only production crons do not run them. Confirm or add these scoped jobs without blindly enabling unrelated schedules. Order reconciliation remains a separate required minutely command. No new queue worker is required for these two commands.

Read-only shipment polling recovers missed shipping/delivery updates for submitted orders from the last 180 days, in bounded batches with a 45-second request-start budget (one final request may finish later). Tracking and pending email delivery continue even when **new checkout or Printful order confirmation is disabled**. Local modes never contact Printful or send these customer emails. Carrier details are shown as supplied; no arrival date is invented when the provider has none. After 180 days, received valid webhooks still update tracking, but automatic polling stops.

`shop_order_emails` is a durable outbox. Normal sends are claimed once; duplicate callbacks or scheduled runs cannot send them again. Disabled email types are marked suppressed rather than backfilled when enabled later. If SMTP delivery is ambiguous, or a sending process disappears, the message becomes **uncertain** and is held instead of being sent repeatedly. Managers can inspect these states under Shop → Orders and use **Retry email** only after checking that the provider did not deliver it. SMTP cannot provide an absolute exactly-once guarantee across a lost acknowledgement; no such guarantee is claimed. Delivery errors retain only their exception class, without addresses or transport bodies.

Reference: [Printful v2 webhook signing and shipment APIs](https://developers.printful.com/docs/v2-beta/).
